**A Wyckoff Consulting Thought Paper**

> **The deal closes. The investment clock starts. The question quickly becomes: who is going to turn the investment thesis into measurable commercial growth?**

The deal closed. The capital is in. The board is aligned. The investment thesis looks compelling.

Now comes the difficult part: execution.

Whether the company is private-equity-backed or venture-backed, investors are not putting capital into a business because they want it to remain exactly where it is today. There is an expectation of value creation: grow revenue, enter new markets, improve margins, professionalize the organization, strengthen the leadership team, create a repeatable commercial engine, build enterprise value, and eventually create a stronger business for the next transaction.

But there is a problem I frequently see in lower-middle-market companies: the growth expectations have changed faster than the marketing organization has.

The company may have been extremely successful getting to $10 million, $20 million, or $40 million. But the marketing infrastructure underneath that success may still look like it did when the company was half its size.

Maybe the founder still drives most business development. Maybe sales owns marketing. Maybe there is a talented marketing manager but no executive marketing leadership. Maybe multiple agencies are operating without a unified strategy. Or perhaps there is essentially no marketing function at all.

The investment thesis says accelerate. The organization is not yet built to do it.

That is where a Fractional Chief Marketing Officer can become an unusually effective tool for investors.

## 1. You Need Executive Marketing Leadership Now—Not Nine Months From Now

Time has a different value in an investor-backed company. If you are working against a three-, five-, or seven-year investment horizon, losing nine months matters.

Yet companies routinely identify a marketing leadership problem and then spend months defining the position, finding candidates, interviewing, negotiating, waiting through notice periods, onboarding, and hoping they hired the right person.

Meanwhile, the investment clock is running. Growth initiatives wait. Sales continues operating without sufficient marketing leverage. Agencies keep spending. New-market opportunities remain unexplored. And the CEO becomes the de facto marketing executive.

A Fractional CMO changes that equation. Experienced executive marketing leadership can enter the business immediately—not simply to keep marketing moving, but to diagnose what the company actually needs.

### Why This Matters to Investors

The first 90–180 days can reveal an enormous amount: where growth is actually coming from, which customers are most profitable, whether the company’s positioning is strong enough, whether the ICP needs refinement, if sales is receiving the support it needs, which marketing investments are producing returns, which should stop, and what capabilities are missing.

It also helps answer a critical hiring question: does the company eventually need a full-time CMO—and if it does, what kind?

Fill the leadership gap immediately. Define the permanent solution intelligently.

## 2. You Need Someone Focused on Enterprise Value—Not Marketing Activity

PE and venture investors do not need more marketing activity. They need business outcomes.

The real questions are bigger than campaigns, websites, social media, and lead generation. How does marketing accelerate revenue? Improve pipeline quality? Enter new verticals? Increase share of wallet? Reduce customer concentration? Improve positioning? Strengthen pricing power? Shorten the sales cycle? Reduce founder dependency? Build a repeatable growth engine? Create a stronger growth story for the next investor?

Those are value-creation questions. Marketing is one of the levers.

### Why This Matters to Investors

A good Fractional CMO should be able to translate the investment thesis into a commercial marketing strategy.

If the thesis calls for geographic expansion, marketing helps determine where and how to enter. If it calls for penetrating new verticals, marketing must validate, position, and build the go-to-market strategy. If it calls for acquisition-driven growth, marketing must help integrate brands and commercial strategies. If it calls for an eventual exit, marketing must help create predictability, differentiation, and a credible future growth story.

The objective is not ‘do better marketing.’ The objective is ‘use marketing to help create a more valuable company.’

## 3. You Need Senior Talent Without Building Senior Overhead Too Early

There is a natural tendency after an investment to professionalize the leadership team. Often that is exactly the right decision.

But not every $10 million, $20 million, or $30 million portfolio company needs another full-time C-suite executive immediately.

A seasoned CMO represents a significant commitment: salary, bonus, benefits, equity, recruiting fees, and—perhaps most importantly—the risk of making the wrong executive hire.

Sometimes the company needs CMO-level thinking far more than it needs a full-time CMO.

That is where the fractional model becomes powerful. The company gains access to an executive who can sit alongside the CEO, participate at the leadership and board level, develop strategy, challenge assumptions, lead the marketing team, manage agencies, align marketing and sales, build measurement systems, recruit talent, create accountability, and drive execution—but the company buys the amount of executive capacity it actually needs.

### Why This Matters to Investors

It creates flexibility. As the business evolves, the model can evolve with it.

Perhaps the Fractional CMO builds the strategy and team. Perhaps they stabilize the organization until a permanent executive is hired. Perhaps they remain the long-term marketing executive because a full-time CMO is not economically necessary. Or perhaps their job is to build the function until the company’s growth justifies bringing the role in-house.

The structure serves the investment thesis—not the organizational chart.

## The Investor's Marketing Question

> **Is the marketing capability of this company sophisticated enough to support the growth assumptions in our investment thesis?**

That question can expose a significant gap. A company may have an excellent CEO, a strong sales team, a great product, loyal customers, healthy margins, and a compelling market opportunity. But if nobody owns the strategy for creating, positioning, and scaling market demand, there is a missing component in the growth engine.

Capital alone does not fix that. Neither does hiring another salesperson. And neither does telling the existing marketing manager to ‘generate more leads.’

Someone needs to own growth from the executive marketing seat.

## What a Fractional CMO Should Do in the First 90 Days

- **Where are we today?** Audit the existing marketing strategy, team, agencies, technology, positioning, spending, and performance.

- **Where is the growth?** Identify the customers, markets, verticals, services, and channels with the greatest potential.

- **What is getting in the way?** Expose weaknesses in positioning, sales alignment, talent, systems, data, and execution.

- **What should we do next?** Build a prioritized marketing roadmap directly connected to the company’s growth plan.

- **How will we measure it?** Create KPIs leadership and investors can use to determine whether marketing is actually contributing to enterprise value.

**Diagnose. Prioritize. Execute. Measure.**

## Three Questions PE and Venture Leaders Should Ask

**1. Does this portfolio company have executive-level marketing leadership capable of delivering the growth assumptions behind our investment?**

Not marketing staff. Marketing leadership.

**2. Are we measuring marketing activity—or business outcomes?**

Website traffic and impressions are interesting. Pipeline, revenue, margin, customer acquisition, market penetration, and enterprise value are considerably more interesting.

**3. Do we actually need a full-time CMO today—or do we need CMO-level capability today?**

Those are not necessarily the same thing.

## The Best Capital Is Still Only as Good as the Execution Behind It

PE and venture capital can create opportunity. It can fund acquisitions, build capacity, hire people, enter markets, develop products, and accelerate growth.

But capital does not create strategy. And capital certainly does not execute it.

That is why marketing leadership should be considered part of the value-creation infrastructure of the business—not simply another corporate function.

Wyckoff Consulting works with established $3 million to $50 million companies at critical business inflection points, including PE- and venture-backed organizations whose growth expectations have outpaced their existing marketing capabilities.

The Fractional CMO model gives investors something particularly valuable: experience without delay, leadership without unnecessary overhead, and strategy tied directly to enterprise value.

Because once the investment is made, the question changes. It is no longer: ‘Why did we buy this company?’ It is: ‘Who is going to help us create the value we bought it for?’

> **You bought the growth story. Now someone has to deliver it.**
